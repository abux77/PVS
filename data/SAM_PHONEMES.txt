PVS SAM PHONEME REFERENCE
=========================
For the native C PVS firmware, September 2026.

This list follows our firmware's PHONEME_NAMES table and parser, rather than
an older manual. Sound descriptions are approximate English listening aids;
pronunciation varies with accent and SAM voice settings.

WRITING A PHRASE
----------------
Use P: for phonemes and S: for ordinary English:

    P:/HEH4LOW WERLD
    S:Hello world

Join phonemes within a word. Separate words with spaces. In PVS, spaces cause
word-by-word rendering and parameter updates, so do not put a space between
every phoneme unless you want each piece treated as a separate word.
Use uppercase for clarity; lowercase phoneme letters are also accepted.
Lines beginning with # are comments in phrase files.

VOWELS
------
IY   Long ee, as in green
IH   Short i, as in fish
EH   Short e, as in bed
AE   Short a, as in cat
AA   Open ah, as in father
AH   Short uh, as in cup
AO   Rounded aw, as in law
UH   Short oo, as in book
AX   Unstressed uh (schwa), as at the start of about
IX   Reduced short i; an unstressed vowel variant
ER   R-colored vowel, as in bird
UX   Alternate oo/u vowel; useful for experimenting with SAM's u sounds
OH   Alternate rounded o vowel; use OW for the usual go diphthong

DIPHTHONGS (VOWELS THAT CHANGE SOUND)
-----------------------------------
EY   ay, as in day
AY   eye, as in my
OY   oy, as in boy
AW   ow, as in now
OW   oh, as in go
UW   oo, as in blue

CONSONANTS
----------
B    b, as in boat
D    d, as in dog
G    Hard g, as in gate
P    p, as in pen
T    t, as in top
K    k, as in kite
CH   ch, as in chip
J    j, as in jam
F    f, as in fan
V    v, as in van
TH   Unvoiced th, as in thin
DH   Voiced th, as in this
S    s, as in sun
Z    z, as in zoo
SH   sh, as in ship
ZH   Voiced zh, as in the middle of vision
/H   h sound, as in hat; the slash is part of the phoneme
/X   Alternate h sound used in SAM's back-vowel contexts
M    m, as in moon
N    n, as in nose
NX   ng, as at the end of sing (not N followed by G)
L    l, as in leaf
R    r, as in red
W    w, as in wet
Y    y, as in yes
WH   Breathier wh variant; WH is valid, HW is not a single phoneme
DX   Quick voiced tap, like the middle consonant of American English butter
Q    Glottal stop, the catch between the two parts of uh-oh

ADDITIONAL CONTEXTUAL VARIANTS
-----------------------------
RX   R variant used after vowels
LX   L variant used after vowels
WX   W-like vowel transition
YX   Y-like vowel transition
GX   Alternate hard-g articulation
KX   Alternate k articulation

The parser may insert/select these automatically. They are also accepted in
phonetic input. Prefer ordinary R, L, W, Y, G and K when starting out.

SYLLABIC CONSONANTS
------------------
UL   Syllabic l, as at the end of bottle
UM   Syllabic m, as at the end of rhythm
UN   Syllabic n, as at the end of button

Our parser expands these into AX followed by L, M or N respectively.

STRESS MARKS
------------
Digits 1 through 8 go immediately AFTER the phoneme they modify, normally a
vowel. For example:

    AY4     AY with stress marker 4
    AE3     AE with stress marker 3
    /HEH4LOW

Stress affects SAM's pitch and phoneme timing; it is not a volume setting.
Start with 4 and listen, then try other markers. Omit the digit for no explicit
stress marker. Do not use 0 or 9, or start a word with a stress digit.

PUNCTUATION
-----------
The phonetic parser accepts:

    .    Period / statement inflection
    ?    Question inflection
    ,    Comma
    -    Dash

Attach punctuation to a word, for example P:/HEH4LOW.
Phonetic punctuation follows the SAM parser/renderer; it is not the same as
PVS's timed punctuation pauses for S: ordinary-text phrases.
Exclamation marks, colons and semicolons are not accepted in P: input.
They can be used in S: input, where PVS handles their pauses.

WHY NEHTIHCS PRODUCED A WARNING
------------------------------
NEHTIHCS can be read as N + EH + T + IH + C + S.
There is no standalone C phoneme. For a hard c sound use K:

    Old:        P:NEHTIHCS
    Valid form: P:NEHTIHKS

That fixes the unsupported letter; it is not a guarantee of the intended
pronunciation. Tune the vowels and stress by ear.
Similarly, COHRPOHREYTIYAXN begins with an unsupported C. Replacing that first
C with K gives KOHRPOHREYTIYAXN, a valid sequence to use as a starting point.

Other common traps:

    H alone      Use /H (or the contextual /X variant).
    C alone      Use K for hard c, or S for soft c.
    X alone      Usually spell the intended sounds, such as KS or GZ.
    NG           Use NX for the single ng sound in sing.
    HW           Use WH if you want that phoneme, or /H followed by W if
                 you deliberately want two separate sounds.
    HWAAN        Contains unsupported H. WAAN is a valid starting point
                 for the w-ah-n sounds; listen and adjust the vowel.

Our compatibility parser skips unsupported letters in some legacy phrases
and prints a warning. That can change pronunciation; fix the spelling rather
than relying on the skipped letters.

COMPLETE INPUT TOKEN CHECKLIST
-----------------------------
Vowels:       IY IH EH AE AA AH AO UH AX IX ER UX OH
Diphthongs:   EY AY OY AW OW UW
Consonants:   B D G P T K CH J F V TH DH S Z SH ZH /H /X M N NX
              L R W Y WH DX Q
Variants:     RX LX WX YX GX KX
Syllabic:     UL UM UN
Punctuation:  . ? , -
Stress:       1 2 3 4 5 6 7 8 (after a phoneme)
Word break:   space

Source-code names like B*, R*, .* and ** contain internal placeholders.
Do NOT type the asterisks. B* means type B; ** is an internal sound component,
not a user-input phoneme. There are no other standalone letter phonemes.

EDITING THE INSTRUMENT
----------------------
Press keys A and D together, then release them. Open the PVS drive and
edit phrases_1.txt through phrases_12.txt as plain text. Save and eject the
drive to resume speech. Each non-comment phrase line occupies a phrase slot.
Keep each phrase shorter than 384 characters, excluding its S: or P: prefix.
